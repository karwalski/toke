## TL;DR
- The thesis that a purpose-built language plus a purpose-built tokenizer is a worthwhile route to token efficiency is partly weakened and partly re-confirmed by a year of evidence: the tokenizer half is real but is being commoditised by superword BPE (SuperBPE, up to 33% fewer tokens with an accuracy gain) and threatened at the root by tokenizer-free architectures (H-Net, Byte Latent Transformer); the strict-compiler half is largely reproducible today with constrained decoding plus type-constrained generation on an existing language, without a new language.
- Syntax-level savings touch only the code fraction of tokens, and that slice is shrinking relative to the two things that actually grew in the past year: input tokens (average prompt length up nearly fourfold since early 2024) and reasoning/agentic output tokens. On realistic agentic workloads a 30 to 52% cut in code tokens nets only single-digit to low-double-digit percent of total token spend, and is dominated by prompt caching, multi-token prediction and reasoning-length control.
- The strongest independent finding against the language route is danluu's 2026 evaluation: the dynamic-vs-static and terse-language token advantages that motivate the thesis largely evaporate on non-trivial tasks at high reasoning effort, and language popularity correlates positively with both correctness and lower cost. The strongest finding for it is that LLMs can learn a novel DSL from an in-context spec at near-native accuracy (Anka: Claude 3.5 Haiku, 99.9% parse success and 95.8% overall task accuracy with zero prior exposure), which lowers the adoption barrier a new language faces.

## What changed since early 2026 (ten lines)
1. Google's monthly token throughput went from roughly 480 trillion (I/O 2025) to over 3.2 quadrillion (I/O 2026), which Sundar Pichai described in the Google I/O 2026 keynote as a "7x" year-on-year jump (about 19 billion tokens per minute), confirming volume, not syntax, as the dominant cost driver.
2. Programming crossed from about 11% to over 50% of token volume on OpenRouter across 2025, and agentic workflows now generate more than half of all output tokens.
3. Superword tokenization (SuperBPE) matured into an ICML 2025 result and faster follow-ups, delivering the bulk of toke's tokenizer gain generically.
4. Tokenizer-free models advanced materially (H-Net dynamic chunking, July 2025; Fast Byte Latent Transformer, 2026), putting the BPE-token-count premise on notice.
5. Diffusion code models went commercial (Mercury, Gemini Diffusion) and open (DiffuCoder, Dream-Coder, LLaDA2), attacking latency and throughput rather than tokens-per-task.
6. Multi-token prediction became a standard inference accelerator (DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3, Nemotron 3), cutting cost per token, not tokens per task.
7. Efficient-reasoning research consolidated (TokenSkip at EMNLP 2025, Chain of Draft, S-GRPO), cutting the fastest-growing slice of output tokens.
8. Type-constrained and grammar-constrained decoding hardened into production infrastructure (XGrammar in vLLM/TensorRT/SGLang; type-constrained code generation at PLDI 2025), reproducing the compile-guarantee benefit on existing languages.
9. Google published the first vendor inference-energy methodology (median Gemini text prompt 0.24 Wh), reframing the energy debate around per-prompt medians and datacentre efficiency, not syntax.
10. Independent evaluation (danluu) and low-resource-language studies undercut the "terse or purpose-built language wins" claim on realistic tasks, while in-context DSL learning (Anka) strengthened the feasibility of adoption.

## The landscape map by lane

### Lane 1: Language level
- **toke (the requester's project), Matthew Watt, active 2026.** Purpose-built language: 13 keywords, 55-character set, LL(1) grammar, LLVM backend, own 16,384-token BPE tokenizer. Maturity: prototype/product in multi-phase development. Evidence quality: self-reported. Self-reported 52% average token reduction versus cl100k across 42 benchmark programs; Gate 1 (held-out 1,000 tasks) 63.7% pass@1, 92.3% compilation, 12.5% token reduction on an 8K vocab; Gate 2 (22 May 2026) 100% compilation pass@1 but only about 8% functional correctness. Relationship: this is the thesis under test. The Gate 2 result is the single most important honest datapoint: the model mastered syntax and compile-correctness but not algorithmic correctness, i.e. the compiler-in-the-loop guarantee is necessary but nowhere near sufficient.
- **Note on the July 2026 "Kern" independent measurement of toke (6,347 cl100k tokens to 3,906 across 60 programs).** I could not independently locate or verify this figure. There are two distinct "Kern/KERN" projects (OscarCode9's KERN-Py, a solo-developer blog concept transpiling to Python, dated 1 August 2026; and unrelated usages), and neither surfaced the 6,347/3,906 numbers. Treat that specific claim as unverified pending a primary link. The implied ratio (about 38.5% reduction) is consistent with toke's own 52%-vs-cl100k claim only if the "Kern" measurement used a different, less favourable baseline or task mix.
- **Martin Alderson, "Which programming languages are most token-efficient?", 8 January 2026 (blog).** RosettaCode across 19 languages via the GPT-4 tokenizer: a 2.6x gap between C (least efficient) and Clojure (most). J at about 70 tokens average; APL only 4th at about 110 because its glyph set tokenises badly ("all those unique glyphs end up as multiple tokens each"). Follow-up (23 February 2026): web frameworks vary by up to 2.9x, mattering more than language. Maturity: opinion/blog with data. Evidence quality: acknowledged by the author as "not a scientific study"; counts existing snippets, not end-to-end task cost. Relationship: superficially supports the thesis, but is the weakest kind of evidence.
- **danluu, "How does programming language affect token efficiency and correctness?", 2026 (blog with original evals).** Ran GPT-5.6-class agents on non-trivial tasks (zstd decoder from spec; Pandoc ProgramBench). Finding: at medium effort the dynamic-language advantage appears, but at ultra effort it disappears, with static languages among the best; obscure/dense languages (J, Assembly) do poorly; language popularity shows a weak-to-moderate positive correlation with both correctness and lower cost. Maturity: opinion/blog. Evidence quality: the best independent, task-level evidence in this lane. Relationship: contradicts the terse-language premise on realistic tasks.
- **memelang, Bri Holt, arXiv 2512.17967 (December 2025).** An "axial grammar" query DSL that an LLM emits and that compiles to parameterised PostgreSQL. Illustrative example: a 22-token Memelang query expands to a 50-token SQL query (about 56% fewer tokens on that example). Maturity: preprint plus working reference implementation. Evidence quality: example-level, not a benchmark suite. Relationship: complements the thesis at the DSL scale (text-to-SQL), where the win is concrete and the target language is verbose.
- **Pel, Behnam Mohammadi, arXiv 2505.13453 (April to June 2025).** A homoiconic language for orchestrating AI agents with a minimal grammar suited to constrained generation and syntax-level capability control. Maturity: design/position paper. Evidence quality: no token benchmarks. Relationship: complements the "small grammar aids constrained decoding" half of the thesis; orthogonal to token counting.
- **sui-lang / Isu, Takato Honda, ~October 2025 (GitHub).** LLM-optimised line-based language transpiling to Python/WASM, since pivoting to "Isu" pseudocode parsed into a closed-vocabulary IR. Maturity: open-source prototype (MIT). Evidence quality: aspirational "100% accuracy" claims, no independent benchmark. Relationship: a direct competitor concept to toke; unproven.
- **Mirror, Austin Z. Henley (CMU), late 2024 (blog + playground).** Programming-by-example: define a signature plus input/output examples, an LLM generates JavaScript. Maturity: proof of concept. Evidence quality: none quantitative. Relationship: orthogonal (not a token-reduction language).
- **Anka, Saif Al Mazrouei, arXiv 2512.23214 (December 2025).** A deliberately verbose DSL for reliable data-transformation code generation, plus a 100-task benchmark. Key result: despite zero prior training exposure, Claude 3.5 Haiku achieved 99.9% parse success and 95.8% overall task accuracy, with a 40 percentage-point advantage over Python on multi-step pipeline tasks (100% vs 60%); GPT-4o-mini cross-validation confirmed +26.7 points on multi-step tasks. Maturity: preprint with released implementation. Evidence quality: author's own suite, but rigorous. Relationship: contradicts toke's terseness instinct (Anka chooses verbosity for reliability) while strengthening the case that a novel language is learnable in-context.

### Lane 2: Tokenizer level
- **SuperBPE, Liu et al., arXiv 2503.13423 (ICML 2025).** Superword tokenizer bridging whitespace: at a fixed 200k vocabulary it encodes text with up to 33% fewer tokens than BPE on average (6.63 vs 4.45 bytes/token), with a +4.0% absolute average gain across 30 downstream tasks (+8.2% MMLU) and 27% less inference compute at 8B scale. Maturity: peer-reviewed with pretrained models. Evidence quality: strong, controlled. Relationship: competes directly with toke's tokenizer half by delivering most of the gain generically, inside standard model training.
- **Faster Superword Tokenization / BoundlessBPE, arXiv 2604.05192 (2026).** Makes superword training practical (BoundlessBPE previously took 4.7 CPU days on 1GB). Maturity: preprint with open implementation. Relationship: accelerates commoditisation of the tokenizer gain.
- **Byte Latent Transformer (BLT), Pagnoni et al., Meta FAIR, ACL 2025 long paper (original December 2024).** Tokenizer-free, entropy-based dynamic byte patching; matches Llama 3 at 8B scale; noted as strongest on code and weak-tokenisation modalities. Maturity: peer-reviewed. Relationship: contradicts the premise that BPE token counts are the right unit.
- **Fast Byte Latent Transformer, arXiv 2605.08044 (2026); HoloByte, arXiv 2603.16917.** Follow-ups improving BLT speed and distillation. Maturity: preprints. Relationship: same as BLT.
- **H-Net dynamic chunking, Hwang, Wang, Gu, arXiv 2507.07955 (July 2025).** End-to-end hierarchical model that learns chunking from raw bytes, matches a transformer of twice its size, and reports nearly 4x data-efficiency improvement on code and DNA. H-Net++ (arXiv 2508.05628) extends to morphologically rich languages. Maturity: preprint with released code and pretrained checkpoints. Evidence quality: strong research result, not yet production. Relationship: the most serious architectural threat to a token-count thesis.

### Lane 3: Output-format level
- **Aider edit formats and benchmarks (aider.chat).** Search/replace and unified-diff formats let the model emit only changes. Per Paul Gauthier's benchmark, the unified diff format raised GPT-4 Turbo from a 20% baseline to 61%, and June GPT-4 from 26% to 59% versus whole-file, while cutting latency and cost. Maturity: adopted product with public benchmark. Evidence quality: strong, widely replicated. Relationship: complements the thesis and captures much of the same output-token saving on an existing language.
- **OpenAI apply_patch (GPT-4.1), Anthropic text editor str_replace, Cursor fast-apply.** Diff/patch tool formats now trained into frontier models. Maturity: products. Relationship: complementary; orthogonal to language choice.
- **Diff-XYZ, arXiv 2510.12487 (October 2025).** A 1,000-item benchmark isolating edit representation: udiff best for apply/anti-apply, search-replace best for diff generation, modified udiff best for small models. Maturity: preprint benchmark. Relationship: complementary.
- **OpenAI Predicted Outputs / speculative edits (November 2024, extended through 2026).** Provide the expected output so the model skips unchanged spans: FactoryAI reported 2 to 4x faster edits (about 70s down to about 20s on large files); Morph and others report 3 to 5x. Critical caveat: OpenAI's own docs state rejected tokens are still billed like normal completion tokens, so this reduces latency and can even raise cost, not token cost. Maturity: product. Relationship: complements agentic editing; reduces latency, not tokens per task.

### Lane 4: Context level
- **LLMLingua family, Microsoft Research (LLMLingua EMNLP 2023, LongLLMLingua ACL 2024, LLMLingua-2).** Prompt compression up to 20x; LongLLMLingua reports 4x fewer tokens with a +17.1% performance improvement in long-context QA. Maturity: adopted open-source. Relationship: attacks input tokens, the larger and faster-growing slice, so it is a competing priority for the same budget.
- **LongCodeZip, arXiv 2510.00446 (2025); CompactPrompt, arXiv 2510.18043.** Code-specific and pipeline context compression. Maturity: preprints. Relationship: complementary to any language choice; targets the code fraction of input directly.
- **Prompt caching economics (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google).** Cached input reads price at roughly 0.1x the uncached rate (OpenAI GPT-5.6-era: cache writes 1.25x, reads 0.1x, 1,024-token minimum). Maturity: product. Relationship: this is the dominant input-cost lever in agentic coding and largely orthogonal to language.
- **Agent context compaction (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Aider).** Repo maps, summarisation and compaction now standard. Maturity: products. Relationship: complementary.
- **Input:output ratio evidence (OpenRouter State of AI, arXiv 2601.10088).** Average prompt length up nearly fourfold since early 2024; completion tokens up roughly threefold; agentic workflows now more than half of output tokens; programming over 50% of volume. Relationship: this data is the crux of question C: input tokens dominate and are growing faster.

### Lane 5: Inference level
- **Efficient reasoning: TokenSkip (arXiv 2502.12067, EMNLP 2025), Chain of Draft, S-GRPO, Train Long Think Short (arXiv 2508.08940).** TokenSkip on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct reduced reasoning tokens by 40% (from 313 to 181 on GSM8K) with less than a 0.4% performance drop; S-GRPO cuts output length 35 to 61%. Maturity: mix of peer-reviewed and preprint. Relationship: attacks the fastest-growing output slice (reasoning), which a language cannot touch. Competes for the same savings goal and usually wins on magnitude.
- **Reasoning-budget controls in frontier models (GPT-5 effort levels, Claude 4.x reasoning budgets).** Maturity: product. Relationship: same as above.
- **Multi-token prediction: DeepSeek-V3 MTP, FastMTP (arXiv 2509.18362), Nemotron 3 (arXiv 2512.20856), Qwen3.** DeepSeek-V3 reports 80 to 90% acceptance for the second token and about 1.8x throughput; SGLang reports up to 60% higher throughput. Maturity: adopted. Relationship: reduces cost/energy per token, not tokens per task. Stacks with, and partly substitutes for, syntax savings on the cost axis.
- **Diffusion code models: Mercury (arXiv 2506.17298), Gemini Diffusion, DiffuCoder (arXiv 2506.20639), Dream-Coder (arXiv 2509.01142), LLaDA2 (arXiv 2512.15745), Seed Diffusion (arXiv 2508.02193), Stable-DiffCoder (arXiv 2601.15892).** Maturity: commercial and open. Relationship: attack latency/throughput via parallel decoding; orthogonal to token counts, but they complicate any BPE-token-count thesis because decoding is no longer strictly left-to-right autoregressive.
- **Speculative decoding, KV-cache compression, distillation to small code models.** Maturity: mainstream. Relationship: cost/energy per token, not tokens per task.

### Lane 6: Verification level
- **XGrammar, Dong et al., arXiv 2411.15100 (integrated into vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM, MLC-LLM); XGrammar 2, arXiv 2601.04426.** Grammar-constrained decoding with up to 100x speedup and near-zero overhead. Maturity: production infrastructure. Relationship: this reproduces toke's "syntactically valid by construction" property on any grammar, including an existing language, without a new language.
- **Type-constrained code generation, Mündler et al., PLDI 2025 (arXiv 2504.09246); CRANE (arXiv 2502.09061); constrained decoding of diffusion LLMs with CFGs (arXiv 2508.10111); Monitor-Guided Decoding.** Type-level and static-analysis-guided constraints reduce compile errors and hallucinated methods. Maturity: peer-reviewed plus preprints. Relationship: this is the crux of question F: constrained decoding plus an existing typed language delivers much of the compile-guarantee benefit toke seeks. Caveat: constraints can reduce reasoning ability if applied naively, and correctness (not just validity) remains unsolved (see toke Gate 2 and the alignment-problem preprint arXiv 2606.21619).
- **Compiler/execution-feedback RL and formal verification in the loop (Lean, Dafny, Verus with LLMs).** Maturity: active research. Relationship: complements iterative coding; the strict compiler in toke's loop is a special case.

### Lane 7: Economics and energy
- **Token volume (Google).** 9.7T (May 2024), roughly 480T (I/O 2025), 980T (July 2025), about 1.3 quadrillion (October 2025), over 3.2 quadrillion (I/O 2026); about 19 billion tokens per minute. Maturity: vendor disclosure. Relationship: establishes that demand growth dwarfs any per-program saving.
- **Coding share.** OpenRouter: programming about 11% to over 50% of volume across 2025 (arXiv 2601.10088). Anthropic Economic Index: coding about 36% of Claude.ai usage (September 2025); Computer/Mathematical 35% of Claude.ai conversations and 44% of API traffic (early 2026); "modifying software to correct errors" alone about 6% of usage. Claude Code run-rate about $500M (September 2025) rising past $2.5B (February 2026). Maturity: vendor reports. Relationship: coding is the single largest slice, so even small percentage savings scale, but see the arithmetic in C.
- **Energy per prompt (Google, arXiv 2508.15734, 21 August 2025).** Median Gemini text prompt 0.24 Wh, 0.03 gCO2e, 0.26 mL water; a 33x energy and 44x carbon reduction per median prompt over 12 months. Maturity: vendor technical report. Relationship: efficiency is already improving by more than an order of magnitude per year from the inference side, swamping syntax gains; and Jevons dynamics (33x efficiency, about 7x volume) mean net energy rose.
- **Cost per solved task: Cost-of-Pass, arXiv 2504.13359 (OpenReview vC9S20zsgN); DoiT "Cost Per Task, Not Cost Per Token" (August 2026).** Maturity: preprint plus industry framework. Relationship: the correct unit of analysis for question B.

### Lane 8: Contradicting evidence
- **Low-resource programming-language penalty (MultiPL-E; MultiPL-T arXiv 2308.09895; TOSEM 2025 survey; no-resource languages arXiv 2606.16827).** Pass@1 for R, Racket, Perl, Swift and Go is at or below 30% versus 50 to 75% for Python, JavaScript and Java (Giagnorio et al., January 2025). Maturity: peer-reviewed and preprint. Relationship: the central risk to any new language: it starts as the lowest-resource language in existence, with no pretraining corpus and no RL environments.
- **In-context DSL learning (Anka, arXiv 2512.23214; Evaluating In-Context Learning of Libraries arXiv 2311.09635; Leveraging Code for Semantic Parsing arXiv 2311.09519).** Mixed but net encouraging: novel DSLs can be parsed near-perfectly from a spec, but rare DSLs are harder to reason in than mainstream languages plus a domain description. Relationship: partially rebuts the low-resource penalty for syntax, not for reasoning.
- **danluu 2026 task-level evals.** As above, the terse-language advantage does not survive non-trivial tasks at high effort. Relationship: the strongest single contradiction.
- **Value of comments and natural-language reasoning.** Multiple efficient-reasoning papers show reasoning tokens materially aid correctness; toke's comment-free, reasoning-light corpus risks trading the wrong thing. Relationship: cautions against stripping natural language.

## Answers to the strategic questions

### A. Has the past year mooted, weakened or strengthened the premise?
Net: weakened on the tokenizer axis, roughly neutral to weakened on the language axis, and strengthened only on adoption feasibility. The tokenizer gain that toke's own baseline called its strongest lever is now available generically: SuperBPE delivers up to 33% fewer tokens with a measured +4.0% average accuracy gain and 27% less inference compute inside ordinary model training, and superword training is now fast enough to be practical. Simultaneously, tokenizer-free architectures (H-Net, BLT and their 2026 follow-ups) are maturing toward the position that the BPE token is not the right unit at all, which would moot token-count-under-BPE comparisons entirely (see D). The one clear strengthening is Anka's result that an LLM can learn a brand-new language from an in-context spec at 99.9% parse success and 95.8% overall task accuracy, which reduces the classic objection that nobody can bootstrap a new language. But danluu's task-level evals and the low-resource-language literature both cut against the idea that a terse or novel language yields a durable correctness or cost advantage on real work.

### B. When is one-shotting at higher token count better than iterating to save tokens?
The evidence points to a clear rule: one-shot a strong model when the failure-and-cleanup cost is high relative to the marginal token cost, and iterate with a cheaper model when acceptance is cheaply checkable (tests, compiler) and retries are near-independent. The Cost-of-Pass framework and the DoiT "cost per solved task" formulation both show that a cheaper model that fails more often can cost more per solved task once you price failed attempts and human cleanup. On compiler-in-the-loop iteration specifically, the evidence is double-edged. toke's Gate 2 (100% compilation but about 8% functional correctness) is the sharpest illustration that a compile guarantee removes only one error class and leaves algorithmic correctness untouched, so compiler iteration alone converges to compiling-but-wrong code. danluu observed that Codex frequently repeats the identical compiler error before fixing it, so faster compiler feedback does not automatically translate into fewer iterations. The compiler-in-the-loop is genuinely valuable, but as an error filter it is necessary and far from sufficient; test or execution feedback is what actually moves functional correctness.

### C. Do syntax-level savings matter for energy and cost at projected volumes? The arithmetic.
Set up a representative agentic coding task with a large context. Assume 100,000 input tokens and 10,000 output tokens (a conservative 10:1 ratio, consistent with prompt lengths having grown nearly fourfold and agentic sessions dominating output). Decompose:
- Output: assume half is code (5,000) and half is reasoning plus tool calls (5,000).
- Input: assume 40% is code (40,000) and 60% is instructions, tool schemas, logs and docs (60,000).

A syntax-level saving under a standard tokenizer only reaches code tokens. Take an aggressive 40% reduction on the code slice:
- Output code: 5,000 to 3,000, saving 2,000.
- Input code: 40,000 to 24,000, saving 16,000 (this requires the model to natively use the terse language on input, i.e. the code already in context is in toke).
- Total saved: 18,000 of 110,000 = about 16.4% of raw tokens.

But two adjustments pull this down. First, if the saving comes from syntax alone under an off-the-shelf BPE tokenizer, realistic per-code reductions versus Python are closer to 20 to 40%, not toke's 52% (which requires toke's own tokenizer and therefore a bespoke model). Second, prompt caching already prices repeated input at about 0.1x, so the input-code slice that dominates the raw arithmetic is often the cheapest slice per dollar. Re-pricing with cached input at 0.1x, the 16,000 input-code tokens saved are worth only about 1,600 token-equivalents, so the economically weighted saving falls to roughly (2,000 + 1,600) / (5,000 + 5,000 + 6,000) = 3,600 / 16,000 = about 22% of the effective billable base, but on a much smaller absolute base, and only if the whole codebase is already in the new language.

Now compare the inference-side levers on the same task: multi-token prediction cuts cost per token by roughly 1.8x throughput (about 44% fewer effective forward passes) across all output tokens; reasoning-length control (TokenSkip 40%, S-GRPO 35 to 61%) cuts the 5,000-token reasoning slice that syntax cannot touch; prompt caching cuts the dominant input slice by about 90%. Each of these attacks a larger or cheaper-to-attack slice than code syntax does.

Energy: at 0.24 Wh median per prompt, and with Google already booking a 33x per-prompt energy improvement in a single year while volume grew about 7x, a syntax-level saving of order 10 to 20% on the code slice is close to a rounding error against both the efficiency trend and the Jevons-driven volume growth. Conclusion for C: syntax-level savings are real but second-order. They are not swamped to zero, but they are dominated by inference-side gains and by input-token growth, and much of their nominal value is already captured by caching.

### D. If tokenizer-free models arrive, what survives?
What does not survive: BPE token counts as the figure of merit, and therefore toke's headline 52%-vs-cl100k comparison and the entire Alderson-style ranking methodology. Under H-Net or BLT the model consumes bytes or learned dynamic chunks, so "tokens per program" ceases to be a stable or meaningful unit, and a terse ASCII syntax loses its tokenizer-arbitrage advantage (BLT is reported strongest exactly where tokenisation is weakest, including code). What survives, and is arguably strengthened: the small LL(1) grammar (it makes grammar-constrained and byte-level generation cheaper and more reliable), the strict compiler as an error filter, and byte-level compactness (fewer bytes still helps a byte-level model, though far less than fewer BPE tokens helped). The strategic implication is that toke should reposition its value proposition away from "fewer BPE tokens" and toward "a grammar and compiler that make constrained, verifiable generation easy," because that is the part robust to an architecture shift.

### E. Fix from both ends: what stacks, what conflicts, where are the gaps?
Stacks cleanly with a purpose-built language: grammar-constrained decoding (XGrammar) and type-constrained generation (a small grammar makes the mask cheaper); diff/patch output formats (orthogonal to language); prompt caching (orthogonal); compiler/execution-feedback RL (the compiler is already in the loop); multi-token prediction and speculative decoding (orthogonal, act on cost per token).
Conflicts or overlaps: a purpose-built tokenizer conflicts with SuperBPE and is mooted by tokenizer-free models; a comment-free, reasoning-light corpus conflicts with efficient-reasoning findings that reasoning tokens aid correctness; terseness conflicts with the Anka/reliability finding that verbosity and one-canonical-form can reduce errors; a bespoke tokenizer conflicts with prompt caching and shared-infrastructure economies because it needs a bespoke model.
Gaps nobody attacked well in the past year: (1) an apples-to-apples, third-party, cost-per-solved-task benchmark comparing a purpose-built language plus bespoke model against a mainstream language plus constrained decoding on the same tasks; (2) end-to-end token accounting for agentic coding that separates code tokens from reasoning, tool-schema and log tokens; (3) whether a small grammar meaningfully improves byte-level (tokenizer-free) generation, which would be toke's best surviving claim; (4) RLVR or execution-feedback training for a purpose-built language to close the syntax-versus-correctness gap that toke Gate 2 exposed.

### F. What should toke borrow, drop or reposition; and the papers that most threaten or support it?
Borrow: constrained decoding (XGrammar/type-constrained generation) as the mechanism, RLVR/execution-feedback training to fix the correctness gap, and diff-format output. Drop or de-emphasise: the BPE-token-count headline as the primary value proposition, and the comment-free/reasoning-light corpus stance. Reposition: from "a more token-efficient language" to "a minimal, LL(1), compiler-verified language designed for constrained and byte-level generation and cheap formal checking," which is robust to SuperBPE and to tokenizer-free models.
Most threatening papers: SuperBPE (arXiv 2503.13423) commoditises the tokenizer gain; H-Net (arXiv 2507.07955) and BLT (ACL 2025) threaten the token-count unit itself; danluu's 2026 evaluation threatens the correctness/cost rationale on real tasks.
Most supportive: Anka (arXiv 2512.23214) shows a novel language is learnable in-context at near-native accuracy; type-constrained code generation (PLDI 2025) and XGrammar (arXiv 2411.15100) show the compile-guarantee benefit is real and cheap on a small grammar.

## Ranked reading list (fifteen most important)
1. SuperBPE: Space Travel for Language Models, arXiv 2503.13423 (ICML 2025). Commoditises toke's strongest lever: up to 33% fewer tokens generically with a +4.0% accuracy gain and 27% less inference compute.
2. Dynamic Chunking for End-to-End Hierarchical Sequence Modeling (H-Net), arXiv 2507.07955 (2025). Tokenizer-free, about 4x data efficiency on code; threatens the token-count premise.
3. Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens, ACL 2025 (original arXiv 2412.09232). Tokenizer-free at 8B scale; strongest where tokenisation is weakest.
4. danluu, "How does programming language affect token efficiency and correctness?", 2026. The best independent task-level rebuttal of the terse-language advantage.
5. Anka: A Domain-Specific Language for Reliable LLM Code Generation, arXiv 2512.23214 (2025). Novel DSL learned in-context at 99.9% parse success and 95.8% task accuracy; the key adoption-feasibility result.
6. Type-Constrained Code Generation with Language Models, PLDI 2025 (arXiv 2504.09246). Delivers the compile-guarantee benefit on an existing typed language.
7. XGrammar: Flexible and Efficient Structured Generation Engine, arXiv 2411.15100. Production grammar-constrained decoding; up to 100x speedup, near-zero overhead.
8. TokenSkip: Controllable Chain-of-Thought Compression, arXiv 2502.12067, EMNLP 2025. Cuts 40% of reasoning tokens (313 to 181 on GSM8K) with under 0.4% accuracy loss.
9. Cost-of-Pass: An Economic Framework, arXiv 2504.13359. The correct unit (cost per solved task) for one-shot-versus-iterate decisions.
10. Measuring the environmental impact of delivering AI at Google Scale, arXiv 2508.15734 (2025). 0.24 Wh median prompt; 33x/44x YoY efficiency; frames the energy debate.
11. State of AI: An Empirical 100 Trillion Token Study with OpenRouter, arXiv 2601.10088 (2026). Programming to over 50% of volume; input tokens up about 4x; agentic dominates output.
12. DiffuCoder: Understanding and Improving Masked Diffusion Models for Code, arXiv 2506.20639 (2025). Anchor for the diffusion-code line that reframes decoding away from autoregression.
13. Insights into DeepSeek-V3 (MTP), arXiv 2505.09343 (2025). MTP at 80 to 90% acceptance, about 1.8x throughput; the cost-per-token lever.
14. Martin Alderson, "Which programming languages are most token-efficient?", 8 January 2026. The reference point for the token-per-language argument (and its limits).
15. Knowledge Transfer from High-Resource to Low-Resource Programming Languages (MultiPL-T), arXiv 2308.09895. Quantifies the low-resource penalty a new language must overcome.

## Gaps and open problems
- No neutral, third-party, cost-per-solved-task comparison of a purpose-built language plus bespoke model versus a mainstream language plus constrained decoding.
- No public decomposition of agentic coding tokens into code, reasoning, tool-schema and log fractions, which is exactly what question C needs.
- Unknown whether a small grammar helps byte-level (tokenizer-free) generation, which would be toke's most durable claim.
- No RLVR/execution-feedback result closing the syntax-versus-correctness gap that toke Gate 2 exposed.
- The July 2026 "Kern" measurement of toke (6,347 to 3,906 tokens) could not be independently verified and needs a primary source.

## Caveats and search limitations
- Several key items in the language lane are blogs or single-developer GitHub projects (KERN-Py, sui-lang, Mirror, Alderson, danluu), used because they are the only evidence; they are labelled as opinion or prototype and should not be read as peer-reviewed.
- All purpose-built-language token-reduction figures, including toke's, are self-reported by their creators and compare across different tokenizers; no neutral benchmark exists.
- Some 2026 arXiv identifiers and model version names in retrieved pages (for example GPT-5.6, Claude Opus 4.x, Nemotron 3, MiMo-V2.5) come from forward-dated sources; where a figure rests on such a source it is attributed to that source rather than presented as established fact.
- My web-search budget was exhausted before I could directly confirm a few items (the sui-lang primary repo, compiler-feedback RL specifics, and the "Kern" toke measurement); these rest on secondary references and are flagged.
- Token-volume figures (Google's 3.2 quadrillion, OpenRouter shares) are vendor or platform disclosures, not audited, and mix modalities; the energy arithmetic uses Google's median-prompt methodology, which excludes training and long queries.
- The cost arithmetic in section C uses stated, illustrative assumptions (10:1 input:output, code fractions, 0.1x cache pricing, 40% code reduction) and is meant to show orders of magnitude, not a precise forecast; different workload mixes will shift the result, but not the qualitative conclusion that syntax savings are second-order.
